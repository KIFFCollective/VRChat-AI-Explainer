01 / DEMAND FOR AN AI LABEL

95% want an AI label, but they disagree on how it should work

The main finding in VRChat's post is that 95% of respondents want a label for AI use. The number tells VRChat very little about what to build, because respondents disagreed on almost every detail of the label.

VRChat gave respondents eight example categories and asked them to build their own labeling scheme, and they came back with 3,141 different combinations. The most popular combination was chosen by fewer than 10% of respondents.

So the 95% agree on a general wish, which is that they want to know what they are walking into before they join a world. They don't agree on a specific design that VRChat could build.

The post reads the 3,141 combinations as a sign that the community is confused. A simpler reading is that the community agrees on the goal and disagrees on the specific solution.

Most respondents want information about AI use, and few would avoid AI worlds

Only a third of respondents would deliberately avoid a world labeled "AI-assisted".

So most of the 95% who want a label would use it as information, and only a minority would use it to filter worlds out. VRChat's decision not to ship AI filters fits its own survey data.

The 37% who said AI is fine as a tool set a condition that sits between banning AI and allowing anything, which is that a person must make the creative decisions. Apart from the creator, almost nobody can check whether a world meets that condition.

03 / AGREED PRIORITIES

Respondents agree most on quality and honest world listings

The survey rated three items as least divisive, including among respondents who use AI themselves. Each item protects players from being misled, and none of them depend on which tools a creator used.

VRChat's plan uses the same three items. It puts accurate thumbnails first, discovery that rewards quality second, and in-game feedback third.

VRChat has near-record player numbers and only 35% positive recent reviews, so players are unhappy but they are not leaving. Steam reviews count the people upset enough to write one, and player numbers count the people who still log in.

The gap shows that the argument over AI has not cost VRChat players so far, whichever side of the argument you take.

The gap also supports the post's central claim, which is that no single group's answer should drive the plan. The group posting the most reviews is smaller than the group still playing.

05 / ENFORCEMENT

VRChat's case depends on enforcement, which the post covers in one paragraph

In the middle of the post, VRChat says: "we have no way to automate detection of something being 'misleading,' so early enforcement has been challenging, and we would face the same enforcement challenge with any type of labeling."

Because VRChat can't detect AI use automatically, every label it could ship would be self-declared, meaning the creator chooses it. A self-declared label is only as accurate as the creators who set it, and the creators with the most reason to lie are the least likely to set it honestly.

VRChat concludes from the enforcement problem that it shouldn't ship a label.

07 / WHAT YOU CAN DO

Actions you can take

01

Answer VRChat's short survey about a "Human-authored" mark

VRChat prefers a "Human-authored" mark over an "AI-generated" label, because saying you used AI can feel like a confession. VRChat still hasn't decided what "Human-authored" should mean. It could mean that the creator used no AI tools at all, or it could mean that a person made all the creative decisions, which is what most respondents cared about.
